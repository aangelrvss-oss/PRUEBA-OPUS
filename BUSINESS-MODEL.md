# BUSINESS-MODEL.md

No revenue, no customers and no pricing tests exist. Everything below is a
hypothesis to test, and it is labelled as one on the landing page.

## Who pays, for what, and why

| Payer | What they pay for | Why they would |
|---|---|---|
| **Entity** (foundation / public-utility association), the treasurer decides, the board approves | Annual plan: donor **certificates** (art. 24 Ley 49/2002, a separate yearly obligation), history kept from year to year, notice when AEAT changes the design | Saves the second-worst January chore (certificates), and the history makes next year's recurrence automatic |
| **Gestoría / asesoría** serving several small entities | Multi-entity plan: several declarants, saved column templates per client | Billable hours saved in the busiest month; fewer complementary returns |
| **Federations and umbrella bodies** (e.g. a regional platform of NGOs) | Licence for members | A member benefit that costs little |

Generating the 182 file stays **free**. It is the acquisition engine, the proof
of quality, and the part that builds trust. Charging for the file would push
treasurers back to the free AEAT form.

## Pricing hypothesis

| Plan | Price (hypothesis) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Gratis | 0 € | File, recurrence, review and history CSVs |
| Entidad | ~49 €/year | Below a gestoría's typical one-off fee for preparing the 182 (UNKNOWN, to verify in interviews) and within a treasurer's discretionary spend |
| Gestoría | ~190 €/year | Several entities; anchored on time saved in January |

Seasonality: almost all value is in December–January. Billing annually, with
the renewal in November, matches that cycle.

## Why this could be sustainable

- Near-zero marginal cost: static hosting, no data storage for the free tier.
- The rules change yearly (RDL 6/2023, Orden HAC/1430/2025): keeping up is the
  recurring value a paid plan sells.
- The paid features that need storage (history kept for you) can be
  end-to-end encrypted to keep the privacy promise.

## Unit economics

UNKNOWN. No acquisition cost or conversion data exist. The cost side is small
and knowable: hosting under 20 €/month; the main cost is one person's time to
update rules each autumn and to support users in January.

## Major risks

1. **Willingness to pay is unproven** and nonprofits are price-sensitive.
2. **Seasonality:** a one-month buying window; a missed January means a lost year.
3. **Liability perception:** a tax-adjacent tool must be right; one bad import
   destroys trust. Mitigation: AEAT validation step, per-donor explanations,
   no auto-filing.
4. **Platform risk:** AEAT could add aggregation and recurrence to its own form.
   That would be good for treasurers and would leave only the certificates and
   gestoría plans.
5. **Small market:** the number of 182 filers is UNKNOWN; this may be a
   niche business, not a venture-scale one. That is acceptable for a
   one-person product.
